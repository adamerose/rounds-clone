# Match the source's jump arc with a general vertical-control change

The source's fighters can stop a jump early. Twenty-six measured source arcs rise anywhere from 48 to 152 pixels over 9 to 27 ticks while leaving the ground at 450–650 px/s in most arcs whatever height they reach, and the tallest of them are as symmetric as the clone's. The clone has one fixed impulse and no way to stop it: `set_player_control` sets `velocity.y = JUMP_SPEED` when jump is pressed while grounded and reads `input.jump` nowhere else, so every clone jump is the same full arc — 116.86 px over 23 ticks, measured through `step` and `snapshot`. That full jump already matches the source's tall arcs in height, duration, take-off speed and descent. What is missing is not a constant — it is the release rule that produces the source's short hops, and its absence is what leaves ticket 049 blocked on its first two bounds.

Add a jump-release cut: when the jump input goes from held to released while the fighter is airborne and still rising, cut the upward velocity by a factor. Change no constant. The airborne condition is load-bearing rather than decorative. Ticket 051 rewrites the seventeen scripted one-tick presses as press-plus-airborne-hold first, and under that rewrite every one of the seventeen releases on a *grounded* tick, so the gate leaves all seventeen arcs untouched by construction rather than by measurement. What the cut can still reach is the release tick of the thirteen multi-tick jump holds the shipped scripts already carry, and the first implementation attempt measured which of those thirteen actually release while their fighter is rising: eight, all in `rematch-draft-replay`. Those eight are extended through their apex before the cut is introduced — an edit that changes no digest today, for exactly the reason ticket 051's rewrite changed none — and with that preparation in place no shipped release is airborne-and-rising at all, so the cut lands with all 73 anchors byte-identical and the first deliberate use of a release cut is ticket 049's ice route.

## Decisions

- Exactly one mechanic is admitted: a release cut on rising vertical velocity while airborne, inside `set_player_control`. No second rule, no coyote time, no variable gravity, no double jump, no air-jump budget and no change to `player_grounded`.
- **The extension comes first, and this ordering is the correction the first implementation attempt bought.** That attempt delivered the mechanic exactly as bound — a once-only cut of ×0.30 on a held-to-released transition while airborne and rising, no constant moved, the release memory private to `PlayerState`, fitted against the retained arcs with per-arc residuals, the continuous variant ruled out — and every anchor behaved as the published set predicted: 58 identical, 15 moved, none outside the movable set of 16. It failed this contract's own no-worse criterion, because the shipped ice route was written against an uncut arc and depends on blue keeping the height the cut removes. With any factor below 1.00 there was no outcome that preserved the round: a sweep over 0.00, 0.20, 0.30, 0.45, 0.60, 0.75, 0.90 and 1.00 through `run_profile_snapshots` found 0.30 and 0.45 send blue off the arena near tick 4740 and hand the ice round to orange, 0.20 and 0.75 leave it unresolved at 5466 with scores [1, 1], 0.60 and 0.90 keep blue's round only because orange leaves the arena instead of being eliminated at 5312, and 0.00 also moves the timber round. Fourteen of the fifteen moved anchors got worse against the source — at `ice-early-traversal` (4786) blue went from 103.5 px away to 489.2 px away and 81.9 px below the bottom edge of the frame — three tests failed and the smoke did not complete. Re-timing that route is ticket 049's scope and is exactly the re-tune this contract forbids, so the fix is not a smaller factor: it is to stage the change so that no shipped release is airborne-and-rising when the cut arrives. The gate itself was never at fault — nought of ticket 051's thirty-three transitions was airborne, as measured — and the mechanic itself is unchanged. The attempt is retained as `out/ticket-050/delivery-attempt-1.patch` with its enumeration, fit, comparison and verification records under `out/ticket-050/delivery-*`; no failing test was committed.
- The guard is not fitted; the cut factor is. The guard is settled by the preservation argument in Outcome — the cut fires only on a held-to-released transition read on an airborne rising tick, because that is the condition under which ticket 051's seventeen rewritten presses provably keep their arcs — and a fit could not have settled it in any case: every measured source arc is airborne from take-off to apex, so the airborne condition is invisible to the residuals. What the fit settles is the factor, and at most one further binary: whether the cut applies once, on the transition tick, or continuously on every airborne rising tick for as long as the input stays released. This contract binds it to **once, on the transition tick**, and the first attempt's measured grid confirms that binding rather than merely asserting it: the continuous variant satisfies none of the four acceptance bands at any factor from 0.10 to 0.60 and any release from nine to twelve ticks after take-off, collapses rise duration (median |Δticks| 2.5–3.5 against once-on-transition's 2.0) and is worse on aggregate ascent residual at every factor above 0.20. Continuous is therefore ruled out, and adopting it would need this contract amended rather than being an implementer's option.
- **The factor is bound to the fitted ×0.30, and the earlier ×0.25 candidate is withdrawn.** ×0.25 came from `clone-jump-model.md`'s integration of the shipped recurrence, which overstates the rise by about 4 %: the model's uncut arc is 122.85 px over 23 ticks where the shipped simulation measured through `step` and `snapshot` gives **116.86 px over 23 ticks**, which is ticket 049's own retained public-trace figure of 116.9 px in 23 ticks. Measured against the shipped simulation, the four acceptance bands are cleared by exactly two factors, both at a release **eleven** ticks after take-off — ×0.20 at 83.27 px / 13 ticks / `v0` 734 / ascent 3040 and ×0.30 at 85.12 px / 14 ticks / `v0` 737 / ascent 3059 — and no factor clears all four at a ten-tick release. ×0.25 clears three and misses fitted `v0` by 2 u/s. Of the two that clear, ×0.30 fits the twenty-four reachable source arcs better on every aggregate: median |Δrise| 0.96 px against 1.54, median |Δv0| 97 against 121 u/s, rms Δv0 149 against 164, median |Δascent| 662 against 793 u/s², rms Δascent 1337 against 1491. The record is `out/ticket-050/delivery-fit.md`, `release-cut-fit.json` and the 552-row grid `release-cut-grid.jsonl`. The implementer re-runs the fit on the final tree against all twenty-six measured arcs — the twenty-three below plus ticket 049's three ice arcs — and reports the per-arc residual. If that re-fit selects ×0.30, it ships. If it disagrees, **both** the re-fit's choice and this contract's ×0.30 are reported with the numbers that separate them, and the re-fit's value ships only if it clears the same four bands; a factor that clears no band is a finding to report, not a value to adopt. **Two of the twenty-six arcs are unreachable at any factor and are named so their absence is not read as a miss**: timber orange PTS 2062658416 (151.5 px in 27 ticks, `v0` 540, ascent 914) and timber orange PTS 2104158250 (129.2 px in 24 ticks, `v0` 904, ascent 2765) both rise above the uncut ceiling of 116.86 px, and a cut can only lower a rise; reaching them needs a higher `JUMP_SPEED`, which this contract freezes.
- Nothing else in vertical control changes. `JUMP_SPEED`, gravity, damping, `dt` and the ascent/descent integration all stay exactly as shipped, because the clone's full jump is already inside the source's tall-arc family: 116.86 px measured against 110.5–151.5, 23 ticks against 18–27, `v_early` 600 inside the source's 450–650 band, descent 1292 u/s² against a source median of 1013 in a 360–1385 range. There is nothing left for a constant to fix.
- `rapier.gravity` is not touched, and this is a scoping decision, not a preference. Gravity is global: the seventeen timber members and their two hanging weights (`insert_timber_structure`, `linear_damping(1.1)`) and the yellow crates (`insert_yellow_crates`, `linear_damping(0.36)`) are ordinary dynamic bodies that set no `gravity_scale` and therefore run at 1.0. Changing gravity moves the timber collapse pile and the crate blast, invalidating `dynamicBodySha256` from the first tick in two profiles whose jump arcs are not in question, and dropping the preserved-anchor count from 32 to about 20. A change confined to `set_player_control` leaves every one of those bodies alone.
- No arena-specific rule and no per-profile special case, restating ticket 049's decision. The change lands for teal, radial, yellow, timber and ice at once. Yellow is unaffected in fact rather than by exception: its script contains no jump input and `PhysicsBoundary::new` gives its fighters `gravity_scale(0.0)`.
- The release memory is authority-internal. Whatever field remembers last tick's jump input must live in the private `PlayerState` and must not be added to `PlayerSnapshot`, `MatchSnapshot`, `CombatMetrics` or any other serialised projection. Ticket 047 measured the cost of getting this wrong: adding `simultaneous_eliminations` to `CombatMetrics` moved `stateSha256` and `combatSha256` on every one of its 37 anchors while every other digest held. A new serialised field here would move every digest of every anchor of every profile and destroy this ticket's entire preservation argument.
- Research options 5 and 6 are rejected. Option 5 — raise the ascent constant instead, scoped to the player body — is contradicted by four independent measurements in the sample below: a 914 u/s² ascent and a 151.5 px rise that a steeper fixed gravity cannot produce, an ascent/descent ratio spanning 0.69 to 11.24 where a fixed multiplier predicts a constant, a modelled 0.06 px parabola residual against the source's measured 1.2–8.4 px, and a `v_early` that stays in the same 450–650 band for tall and short jumps alike where the model predicts it falling to 526–538. Option 6 — change `rapier.gravity` — has no supporting evidence at all and is the only option that also moves the timber pile and the crates; it is named here so it stays ruled out. Option 3, ticket 051 followed by this cut, is what this contract implements; option 2 is now ticket 051; option 4 is this change without the split, which is the risk-5 shape this ticket used to carry; option 7's probe is folded into the required fit table.
- Depends on ticket 051. Without it, seventeen scripted presses read as released on their first tick and forty-one anchors move for no product reason. This contract's preservation claims are stated against the post-051 scripts.
- Cargo must reuse the prepared target at `out/cargo-target` under the repository two-job cap in `.cargo/config.toml`; no clean build and no concurrent Cargo invocation. Any visible window is the automated `visible-flow` run alone, launched hidden with its verified centre inside monitor 4 before it is shown.
- Risk is 3: the movable anchor set is empty, the extension that empties it is provable as a zero-change edit before the cut exists, the factor is already fitted at the public boundary and the mechanic is already written, measured and retained as a patch, so the honest remaining exposure is that the cut reaches shared vertical control in all five arenas in one commit — but nothing shipped exercises it until ticket 049 re-times the ice route, and any mistake in it surfaces as a moved digest against a 73-of-73 identical gate rather than as changed product behaviour.

### Method, and why a camera measurement was needed first

Frame index is the true tick offset `(pts − pts0) / 166666`, not the position in the detected-row list, and a phase is cut wherever more than one consecutive frame is missing, so the handful of undetected frames do not compress the time axis.

The take-off, apex and landing frames of the teal, radial and timber arcs were inspected at 1280×720 (`out/ticket-050/frames/`): each shows the fighter leaving a surface, airborne at the apex with the arena landmarks in identical screen positions, and the camera unmoved.

**What recurs.** A short, steep rise followed by a gentler fall recurs everywhere. Across the 23 arcs the median rise is 87.9 px in 15 ticks, the median fitted ascent is 2822 u/s² and the median fitted descent, over the nine arcs with a fall of at least twelve ticks, is 1013 u/s². The median ascent/descent ratio is 2.73 against the clone's 1.3, so the ice's "about 3.0" is a fair central value for the source in general.

**What does not recur, and this is the finding.** The ratio is not a constant: it runs from 0.69 to 11.24 across arcs measured with the same tracker on a static camera. Rise height varies by a factor of 3.16 (48.0 to 151.5 px) and rise duration by a factor of 3 (9 to 27 ticks). Fitted ascent acceleration varies by a factor of 7.25 (914 to 6622 u/s²) while fitted descent acceleration varies by 3.85 (360 to 1385), seven of its nine values falling between 698 and 1385. No single vertical constant can produce both a 914 u/s² ascent and a 6622 u/s² ascent.

**The jumps held to full height exist, and they are symmetric.** Timber orange PTS 2062658416 rises 151.5 px over 27 ticks at a fitted ascent of 914 u/s², against its own fall's 1323; timber orange PTS 2069825054 rises 104.0 px over 19 ticks at 974 against its own fall's 925. Those two arcs have essentially no ascent/descent asymmetry at all. The short arcs are the asymmetric ones: 72.0 px in 9 ticks gives 6622 against 535, and 74.5 px in 11 ticks gives 3801 against 510. `corr(rise ticks, ascent a) = −0.56`. That is the pattern a cut rise produces when a single parabola is fitted over it, and it is not the pattern a fixed steeper gravity produces.

**The take-off impulse itself does not vary with the rise.** `v_early` has median 525 u/s with most arcs between 450 and 650, and `corr(rise px, v_early) = +0.01`. Fighters leave the ground at the same speed and stop rising at different times. Under a fixed short steep jump the two would move together.

**A third, weaker line of evidence.** Over the nine paired arcs the ascent fits a single parabola worse than its own descent in eight cases (median ascent rms 3.93 px against descent rms 2.08 px), and ticket 049's three ice arcs show the same ordering (2.64/3.21/4.39 against 1.50/2.20). Tracking noise is identical on both phases, so the ascent is the phase that is not a single constant acceleration.

The recordings therefore contain jumps the fighter clearly held longer and that rise much higher — the two timber arcs above, at 151.5 px and 104.0 px, against a 48.0 px minimum in the same arena and a 72.0 px arc by the same fighter fifteen seconds later. The source has a variable-height jump. The 12–13 tick rise measured at the ice edge is one point in a 9–27 tick distribution, not a constant.

### Why ticket 051 comes first

`grounded_windows.py` walked the public `grounded` flag for 48 ticks after every one-tick press (`out/ticket-050/grounded-windows.json`), and `out/ticket-051/flight_ends.py` closed the four windows that had not re-grounded inside that span. `grounded` stays true for zero to eighteen ticks *after* a press, so a press cannot simply be lengthened into a hold — the impulse re-applies, exactly as ticket 049 recorded for orange at 4711/4712. But `set_player_control` ignores `input.jump` entirely while airborne, so a press can be rewritten as `{T} ∪ [first airborne tick, first re-grounded tick)` and today's digests do not change at all, while under a release rule that jump is then fully held and its arc does not change either.

Seven of the seventeen presses have a contiguous airborne run longer than the 23-tick rise and become free full holds: teal blue 40, 500 and 650, timber orange 120, timber blue 260, rematch orange 4929 and rematch blue 5000. Nine have one to seven airborne ticks and stay short whatever they are given: radial orange 160, 340, 520, 820 and 888, radial blue 650, timber orange 1280, timber blue 1150 and rematch orange 5211. One — timber orange 960 — reports grounded on all 48 ticks after its press and can hold nothing at all. Under the airborne gate none of the seventeen, those ten included, can be cut: every one of them releases on a grounded tick, as Outcome shows from 051's own `{T} ∪ [A, R)` rule. This ticket's anchor movement therefore comes only from the thirteen pre-existing multi-tick holds listed in Outcome, none of which ticket 051 touches. The exact windows for the seventeen are tabulated in ticket 051.

### Still open, carried forward

- Ticket 049's unexplained level stretch — source blue's vertical velocity near zero for eleven frames, ticks 4726–4736, fitting 33 u/s upward with 214 u/s² against nothing beneath it — is untouched by this research and is not explained by a release cut. A two-constant ascent/descent model will not reproduce it either.
- Whether the cut applies once or continuously is **no longer open**: the measured grid clears none of the four acceptance bands for the continuous variant at any factor from 0.10 to 0.60 or any release from nine to twelve ticks, so Decisions binds it to once-on-transition and adopting continuous would need an amendment. Whether the cut fires only while airborne was never open: it is settled in Outcome, and with it the fact that radial's five near-surface hops and timber orange 960 do not change at all.
