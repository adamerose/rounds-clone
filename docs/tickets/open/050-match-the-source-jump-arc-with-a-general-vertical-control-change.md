# Match the source's jump arc with a general vertical-control change

The source's fighters can stop a jump early. Twenty-six measured source arcs rise anywhere from 48 to 152 pixels over 9 to 27 ticks while leaving the ground at the same 450–650 px/s every time, and the tallest of them are as symmetric as the clone's. The clone has one fixed impulse and no way to stop it: `set_player_control` sets `velocity.y = JUMP_SPEED` when jump is pressed while grounded and reads `input.jump` nowhere else, so every clone jump is the full 122.85 px over 23 ticks. That full jump already matches the source's tall arcs in height, duration, take-off speed and descent. What is missing is not a constant — it is the release rule that produces the source's short hops, and its absence is what leaves ticket 049 blocked on its first two bounds.

Add a jump-release cut: when the jump input goes from held to released while the fighter is airborne and still rising, cut the upward velocity by a factor. Change no constant. The airborne condition is load-bearing rather than decorative. Ticket 051 rewrites the seventeen scripted one-tick presses as press-plus-airborne-hold first, and under that rewrite every one of the seventeen releases on a *grounded* tick, so the gate leaves all seventeen arcs untouched by construction rather than by measurement. What the cut can still reach is the release tick of the thirteen multi-tick jump holds the shipped scripts already carry, and that is what turns this from a change that perturbs forty-one anchors into one that can perturb at most twenty, enumerated before a single frame is recaptured.

## Outcome

- `set_player_control` cuts the fighter's upward velocity by a fixed factor on the tick its jump input transitions from held to released **while that fighter is airborne and still rising** — the `grounded` argument the function is already passed is false on that tick, and `velocity.y > 0` — and does nothing on any other tick. A release read on a grounded tick does nothing at all, whatever the vertical velocity. `JUMP_SPEED` (680), `rapier.gravity` (0, −1500), the fighter's `linear_damping` (0.7), `AIR_CONTROL`, `RUN_SPEED` and the fixed `dt` of 1/60 are all byte-identical to today. A held jump behaves exactly as it does now.
- Source-shaped short hops are reachable through ordinary scripted input. Demonstrated at the public step/snapshot boundary: a press released after about ten ticks produces a rise of 82–90 px over 12–14 ticks whose whole-phase constant-acceleration fit gives `v0` in 730–780 u/s and an ascent of 2900–3300 u/s², against the source's measured ice hop of 82.5 px in 13 ticks fitting `v0` 746.5 and ascent 3100 u/s² (**Measured basis**, and `out/ticket-049/source-blue-track.json`). A jump held through its whole rise still reaches 122.85 px in 23 ticks.
- **All seventeen of ticket 051's rewritten presses keep their arcs by construction, and none of them can ever be cut.** 051 rewrites each press `{T}` as `{T} ∪ [A, R)`, where `A` is the first tick after `T` on which that fighter's `PlayerSnapshot.grounded` is false and `R` is the first tick after `A` on which it is true again. Each rewritten row therefore carries exactly two held-to-released transitions — one at `T + 1`, because `A > T + 1` in every one of the seventeen rows of 051's table, and one at `R` — and both fall on grounded ticks by 051's own definitions: ticks `T + 1` through `A − 1` are grounded because `A` is the *first* airborne tick, and `R` is by definition a re-grounded tick. The airborne gate cannot fire on either, so the seventeen keep their arcs whatever cut factor is chosen. Timber orange 960, which gains no hold at all and stays a bare one-tick press, releases at 961 with `grounded` true on every one of the 48 ticks after its press. The four fighters that leave the ground again later inside the same probe do so with their jump input already false, so those later flights contain no held-to-released transition to cut. This is the argument the whole contract rests on; it needs no fit and no capture, and the regression asserts it directly.
- **The only jumps this cut can reach are the multi-tick jump holds the shipped scripts already carry and that ticket 051 does not touch.** There are thirteen, read straight out of `scripted_inputs_for` and `connected_ice_input`, and they live in two profiles only. ## Decisions

- Exactly one mechanic is admitted: a release cut on rising vertical velocity while airborne, inside `set_player_control`. No second rule, no coyote time, no variable gravity, no double jump, no air-jump budget and no change to `player_grounded`.
- The guard is not fitted; the cut factor is. The guard is settled by the preservation argument in Outcome — the cut fires only on a held-to-released transition read on an airborne rising tick, because that is the condition under which ticket 051's seventeen rewritten presses provably keep their arcs — and a fit could not have settled it in any case: every measured source arc is airborne from take-off to apex, so the airborne condition is invisible to the residuals. What the fit settles is the factor, and at most one further binary: whether the cut applies once, on the transition tick, or continuously on every airborne rising tick for as long as the input stays released. This contract binds it to **once, on the transition tick**, unless the fit residuals demand otherwise, and the implementer must report both the chosen factor and whether the once-only application survived. The candidate factor is ×0.25, because the shipped recurrence with a release at ten ticks and a ×0.25 cut reproduces the ice hop's 82.5 px / 13 ticks / `v0` 746.5 / ascent 3100 as 86.58 px / 13 ticks / `v0` 750 / ascent 3046 (**Measured basis**, `out/ticket-050/clone-jump-model.json`). The implementer fits the factor against the twenty-three measured arcs in the table below plus ticket 049's three ice arcs, reports the residual per arc, and either confirms ×0.25 or replaces it with the fitted value and says why.
- Nothing else in vertical control changes. `JUMP_SPEED`, gravity, damping, `dt` and the ascent/descent integration all stay exactly as shipped, because the clone's full jump is already inside the source's tall-arc family: 122.85 px against 110.5–151.5, 23 ticks against 18–27, `v_early` 600 inside the source's 450–650 band, descent 1292 u/s² against a source median of 1013 in a 360–1385 range. There is nothing left for a constant to fix.
- `rapier.gravity` is not touched, and this is a scoping decision, not a preference. Gravity is global: the seventeen timber members and their two hanging weights (`insert_timber_structure`, `linear_damping(1.1)`) and the yellow crates (`insert_yellow_crates`, `linear_damping(0.36)`) are ordinary dynamic bodies that set no `gravity_scale` and therefore run at 1.0. Changing gravity moves the timber collapse pile and the crate blast, invalidating `dynamicBodySha256` from the first tick in two profiles whose jump arcs are not in question, and dropping the preserved-anchor count from 32 to about 20. A change confined to `set_player_control` leaves every one of those bodies alone.
- No arena-specific rule and no per-profile special case, restating ticket 049's decision. The change lands for teal, radial, yellow, timber and ice at once. Yellow is unaffected in fact rather than by exception: its script contains no jump input and `PhysicsBoundary::new` gives its fighters `gravity_scale(0.0)`.
- The release memory is authority-internal. Whatever field remembers last tick's jump input must live in the private `PlayerState` and must not be added to `PlayerSnapshot`, `MatchSnapshot`, `CombatMetrics` or any other serialised projection. Ticket 047 measured the cost of getting this wrong: adding `simultaneous_eliminations` to `CombatMetrics` moved `stateSha256` and `combatSha256` on every one of its 37 anchors while every other digest held. A new serialised field here would move every digest of every anchor of every profile and destroy this ticket's entire preservation argument.
- Research options 5 and 6 are rejected. Option 5 — raise the ascent constant instead, scoped to the player body — is contradicted by four independent measurements in the sample below: a 914 u/s² ascent and a 151.5 px rise that a steeper fixed gravity cannot produce, an ascent/descent ratio spanning 0.69 to 11.24 where a fixed multiplier predicts a constant, a modelled 0.06 px parabola residual against the source's measured 1.2–8.4 px, and a `v_early` that stays in the same 450–650 band for tall and short jumps alike where the model predicts it falling to 526–538. Option 6 — change `rapier.gravity` — has no supporting evidence at all and is the only option that also moves the timber pile and the crates; it is named here so it stays ruled out. Option 3, ticket 051 followed by this cut, is what this contract implements; option 2 is now ticket 051; option 4 is this change without the split, which is the risk-5 shape this ticket used to carry; option 7's probe is folded into the required fit table.
- Depends on ticket 051. Without it, seventeen scripted presses read as released on their first tick and forty-one anchors move for no product reason. This contract's preservation claims are stated against the post-051 scripts.
- Cargo must reuse the prepared target at `out/cargo-target` under the repository two-job cap in `.cargo/config.toml`; no clean build and no concurrent Cargo invocation. Any visible window is the automated `visible-flow` run alone, launched hidden with its verified centre inside monitor 4 before it is shown.
- Risk is 4, and with the airborne gate that number is defensible rather than hopeful: the gate makes all seventeen of ticket 051's presses provably uncuttable from 051's own construction instead of from a measurement, which caps the movable set at the thirteen pre-existing hold releases and at most twenty of the 73 anchors, the implementer publishes the identical set before capturing so any surprise is a defect and not a re-tune, a moved anchor that gets worse against the source blocks delivery outright, and the whole mechanic is one branch in one function that reverts exactly against arc claims falsifiable by twenty-six source arcs whose frames are retained by PTS and native RGBA SHA-256. It is not lower than 4 because the cut factor is still fitted from a model rather than read off the footage, because the mechanic reaches shared vertical control in all five arenas in one commit, and because up to twenty source-paired anchors may genuinely need recapture.

### Method, and why a camera measurement was needed first

The decoder is ticket 049's pipeline, generalised only in taking the source path so it can also read the second recording: imageio-ffmpeg's bundled FFmpeg 7.1, `-copyts`, `-fps_mode passthrough`, `format=rgba`, `showinfo` for integer PTS, SHA-256 over exactly 3,686,400 native 1280×720 RGBA bytes. `out/ticket-050/decode_frames.py` writes frames and hashes; `track_stream.py` consumes the same stream in memory so a 24-second interval can be tracked without caching gigabytes. Each fighter is measured by the rigid green health bar (~`(158,213,42)`) that ticket 049 used, because the translucent motion trail moves a raw blob centroid by tens of pixels; the bar's owner is decided per frame by whether a blue or an orange body mask dominates the window below it, so neither fighter's screen side is assumed. Frame index is the true tick offset `(pts − pts0) / 166666`, not the position in the detected-row list, and a phase is cut wherever more than one consecutive frame is missing, so the handful of undetected frames do not compress the time axis.

Ticket 049 could take the ice camera as static. These four intervals cannot, so two independent estimators were built and cross-checked. `camera_track.py` phase-correlates a 6×4 grid of 160 px patches on a gradient-magnitude image against the interval's first frame and fits a uniform zoom plus translation about the frame centre, discarding the worst 40 % of patches. `landmark_camera.py` measures one large rigid arena landmark per arena — the teal cyan/lime platform faces, the radial dark diamond enclosure, the timber hot-pink floor, the yellow platform faces — as robust percentile bounds and derives the same zoom and translation from them.

The take-off, apex and landing frames of the teal, radial and timber arcs were inspected at 1280×720 (`out/ticket-050/frames/`): each shows the fighter leaving a surface, airborne at the apex with the arena landmarks in identical screen positions, and the camera unmoved.

**What recurs.** A short, steep rise followed by a gentler fall recurs everywhere. Across the 23 arcs the median rise is 87.9 px in 15 ticks, the median fitted ascent is 2822 u/s² and the median fitted descent, over the nine arcs with a fall of at least twelve ticks, is 1013 u/s². The median ascent/descent ratio is 2.73 against the clone's 1.3, so the ice's "about 3.0" is a fair central value for the source in general.

**What does not recur, and this is the finding.** The ratio is not a constant: it runs from 0.69 to 11.24 across arcs measured with the same tracker on a static camera. Rise height varies by a factor of 3.16 (48.0 to 151.5 px) and rise duration by a factor of 3 (9 to 27 ticks). Fitted ascent acceleration varies by a factor of 7.25 (914 to 6622 u/s²) while fitted descent acceleration varies by 3.85 (360 to 1385), seven of its nine values falling between 698 and 1385. No single vertical constant can produce both a 914 u/s² ascent and a 6622 u/s² ascent.

**The jumps held to full height exist, and they are symmetric.** Timber orange PTS 2062658416 rises 151.5 px over 27 ticks at a fitted ascent of 914 u/s², against its own fall's 1323; timber orange PTS 2069825054 rises 104.0 px over 19 ticks at 974 against its own fall's 925. Those two arcs have essentially no ascent/descent asymmetry at all. The short arcs are the asymmetric ones: 72.0 px in 9 ticks gives 6622 against 535, and 74.5 px in 11 ticks gives 3801 against 510. `corr(rise ticks, ascent a) = −0.56`. That is the pattern a cut rise produces when a single parabola is fitted over it, and it is not the pattern a fixed steeper gravity produces.

**The take-off impulse itself does not vary with the rise.** `v_early` has median 525 u/s with most arcs between 450 and 650, and `corr(rise px, v_early) = +0.01`. Fighters leave the ground at the same speed and stop rising at different times. Under a fixed short steep jump the two would move together.

**A third, weaker line of evidence.** Over the nine paired arcs the ascent fits a single parabola worse than its own descent in eight cases (median ascent rms 3.93 px against descent rms 2.08 px), and ticket 049's three ice arcs show the same ordering (2.64/3.21/4.39 against 1.50/2.20). Tracking noise is identical on both phases, so the ascent is the phase that is not a single constant acceleration.

The recordings therefore contain jumps the fighter clearly held longer and that rise much higher — the two timber arcs above, at 151.5 px and 104.0 px, against a 48.0 px minimum in the same arena and a 72.0 px arc by the same fighter fifteen seconds later. The source has a variable-height jump. The 12–13 tick rise measured at the ice edge is one point in a 9–27 tick distribution, not a constant.
