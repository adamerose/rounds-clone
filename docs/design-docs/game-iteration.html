Make the fight worth playing

Agents diagnose, test and deliver improvements without asking Adam to choose tuning numbers.

His 14 October 2026 feedback replaces feature-count completion with a game the group wants to repeat, followed by a dependable Steam release.

Who decides

Agents own reversible tuning, usability fixes, audiovisual iteration, experiments and implementation within the settled direction.

Adam tries the best build at occasional checkpoints and gives a brief preference or the worst moment. He need not specify movement or bullet values.

Ask for new product direction, conflicting outcomes, spending, account/legal authority or public publication. Prepare the concrete result first.

The AFK practice default and detailed numbers were explicit requests and require no further approval.

The shortest useful loop

  1. State one observed problem and a falsifiable explanation. Keep a playable baseline and its content identity.
  2. Measure through legal inputs: movement, reversal, stop, jump, flight/drop, block, recoil or the reported bad moment.
  3. Try at most three meaningful candidates. Change one related parameter family after the initial corrective pass.
  4. Screen for regressions, readable output and bad tradeoffs. Reject faster running that introduces slippery stopping.
  5. Promote the strongest provisional candidate after independent review. Retain the previous build for comparison or rollback.
  6. At a meaningful checkpoint offer a short playable comparison: which felt better, and what remained worst? Record actual feedback.
  7. Choose the next problem from that evidence. Stop a family when comparisons cease to show useful improvement; reopen it only with new evidence.

Do not require weekly appointments or repeated parameter-ranking chores. Bring a focused improvement and one experience question.

Keep evidence distinct

Correctness
Legal inputs, deterministic recordings, regressions and network agreement establish rules and recovery.
Experienced behavior
Trajectories and rendered frames establish actual speed, travel, drop, stopping, visibility and timing under the tested conditions.
Human preference
Players report responsiveness, agency, satisfaction and frustration. Voluntary rematches support the claim that play is worth repeating.

A passing smoke or model description cannot prove that combat is fun. Metrics diagnose problems and screen regressions; they are not a synthetic fun score.

Practice is the entry point

Launch into one controlled player and an idle target. Start with base combat, automatically restore the target, and expose reset and the online menu.

Detailed mode separates configured numbers and observed motion. The controller's target run speed is not the achieved ground speed.

Exact card modifiers and reaction values come from the actual catalog. Remote tuning must come from the host, not a client's local file.

Reuse the simulation

PlayerInput and AuthoritativeMatch own legal control and rules. InputRecording pins content; replay yields snapshots; capture renders them.

The first measurements use recording prefixes through the existing client. No agent framework, model call per tick or optimizer is required.

Add sampled replay output and human input recording when repeated evidence collection warrants the work.

Derive movement/flight/inactivity from snapshots outside private physics rather than duplicating collision rules.

Use several bounded policies only when a question needs them. Delayed observation and imperfect aim are hypotheses to calibrate, not certified human behavior.

The smoke policy has exact aim and only one firing fighter. Keep it as a correctness check, never a balance or enjoyment test.

Add no general event bus for polish until existing impacts, counters and observed edges demonstrably fail an audio/effect consumer.

Quality gates

The roadmap orders base response/feedback, complete online matches, onboarding/reliability and Steam release.

Sound and readable firing, hit, block, reload and death feedback precede content expansion: they let players understand and evaluate the fight.

Keep existing cards and arenas playable. New content follows a demonstrated gap after the base experience is stable.

Steam readiness includes installation, performance, settings, account identity, licenses, store/build review and support alongside game quality.

Research and its limits

Exploring Gameplay With AI Agents uses automated exploration to answer designer questions.

It supports bounded scenario measurements here, not a universal test of enjoyment.

Synthetic and human-like test agents evaluates bug discovery from generated and human-derived trajectories.

It distinguishes defect discovery from preference; it does not establish that our simple scripted agents model people.

LLM game-difficulty testing studies Wordle/Slay the Spire and excludes heavily visual action games.

Those findings do not validate judging this physics duel's real-time feel from language-model play or screenshots.

AI engagement/difficulty prediction calibrates predictions against human behavior in its studied game.

Our uncalibrated motion probes have no equivalent enjoyment claim.

Steam Playtest provides a separate child App ID and controlled player access.

Use it for external feedback once account/product prerequisites exist. Local automation cannot perform a two-home social playtest.
